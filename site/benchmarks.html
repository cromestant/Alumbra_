Benchmarks

What we measured while building Alumbra, and what changed because of it. Samples are small (this was built in a week), so each result says how much data is behind it.

Summary. The eval suite passes 22 to 25 of 27 questions per run, with a median of about 25 seconds per ask. Most of an ask's time is the answer step and the object searches. The worst delays come from rare single searches that hang, which a backup copy now caps. The second model on Baseten is faster than Spark on short reads but makes more labelling mistakes.

On this page: Eval suite · Spark vs Baseten races · Latency profile · Slow searches and backups · Naming comparison · First live asks

2. Spark vs Baseten races

In three places Alumbra sends the same request to Spark and to a second model, Muse Spark Open on Baseten, and uses whichever good answer comes first within its time limit. To see how that plays out, we ran 7 real asks and let both sides of every race run to completion (November 2, 2026).

3. Latency profile of one ask

A count question ("How many mailbox banks are there?") once took 145 seconds in a benchmark run. We timed every step of the same ask, repeated it, and it took 28 to 33 seconds each time. Its eval history is 15 to 32 seconds, with one 82-second run. The 145 seconds was a rare slow call, not a fixed cost.

StepRun 1Run 2
Naming (Spark)5.6 s6.2 s
Object searches (SAM, 15 to 16 in parallel)8.5 s17.5 s
Count search (SAM)4.2 s1.9 s
Safety gate (Jev)0.3 s0.2 s
Answer (Spark)9.3 s7.3 s
Total27.9 s33.2 s

Two findings changed the code:

  • The count search waited for nothing. It needs only the photo and the question, but ran after all other searches. It now starts with the ask and finishes during naming, saving 2 to 4 seconds on every count question.
  • One slow search holds the whole ask. In run 2 a single search ("curb") took 11.9 seconds while the rest took 2 to 5. That led to the backup below.

Every ask now logs the time of each step, so slow asks can be diagnosed from the logs.

4. Slow searches and backups

Across 61 SAM searches from four profiled asks: median 2.6 seconds, 90th percentile 5.4 seconds, slowest 11.9 seconds. Separately, one search on a test phone took 92.6 seconds and turned a normal ask into a 135-second one.

The fix: if a search hasn't answered after a set time, send an identical copy and use whichever answers first. The question was where to set that time, since a copy that loses is a wasted call.

Send a copy afterCopies sentCopies that would win
5 s7 of 61 (11%)1
6 s5 of 61 (8%)1
8 s (chosen)1 of 61 (2%)1

At 5 seconds most copies would lose to originals finishing at 5 to 7 seconds. At 8 seconds only the genuinely stuck search gets a copy, about 2% extra searches, and a 90-second hang becomes about 11 seconds. A live check with the time forced down to 2 seconds sent 11 copies and none won, which confirms that a short limit only wastes calls.

5. Naming: Spark vs Baseten

Could the faster Baseten model name scenes instead of Spark? Both got the same prompt, at the same time, on 10 eval photos, twice each (November 2, 2026). One photo with readable personal labels was left out.

ModelMedianMaxErrors
Spark7.4 s11.1 s0 of 20
Baseten6.3 s7.5 s0 of 20
  • Baseten was faster in 17 of 20 pairs, but only by about a second on full photos.
  • The two lists differ about as much as two Spark runs do. Exact-phrase overlap was 0.31 between the models and 0.33 between two Spark runs on the same photo. Most differences are the same object named differently ("fallen" vs "scattered" ice cubes).
  • Each model misses things the other finds. On a sidewalk photo Spark named a fire hydrant and a trash bin in both runs, Baseten in one. Baseten alone found a charging cable, a puddle and wet footprints.
  • No invented objects appeared in either list.

Result: not adopted for now. Combining both lists is on the roadmap.

6. First live asks

The first ask through the public server (November 2, 2026): "Where is my ragdoll?", spoken over a short video clip. 26.0 seconds end to end.

The answer, "Your ragdoll is at your 10 o'clock, to the left, a few steps away. It is on the chair beside the door", was correct. But SAM had not found the cat under the word "ragdoll", so that position came from Spark looking at the photo, not from a measured box. Falling back to a general word ("cat") when the specific one finds nothing is on the roadmap.

Method notes

  • Latency depends on the model services' load at the time; the same ask varied by several seconds between runs.
  • The race and naming results come from one session each. They show direction, not stable rates.
  • Eval photos are real photos taken by the developer. They are not published.
