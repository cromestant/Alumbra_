Volunteer escalation

AI handles the everyday; people handle the hard. Some questions should never be decided by a model alone. For those, Alumbra hands the question to a human volunteer and keeps the user informed while it waits.

Which questions are escalated

Question typeExampleWhat happens
Crossing a street, or a traffic signal's state"Can I cross now?" "Is the light green?"Escalated. Alumbra describes what it sees but never tells you to go.
Taking a dose of medication"Can I take two of these now?" "What's the dose?"Escalated.
Eating or drinking something"Is this safe to eat?"Not escalated. A fixed reply: Alumbra can't tell from a photo whether it's OK for you, so check with someone who knows.
Everything else"Where are my keys?" "Is the path clear?"Answered by Alumbra, with hazard warnings.

Reading a medicine label ("which one is the ibuprofen?") is answered normally. Only the decision to take a dose goes to a person.

How the decision is made

Two independent layers check every question, and either one is enough to escalate:

  1. Word patterns in the code, such as "cross" together with "street", "now" or "safe"; "can I take", "what's the dose", "side effects"; "eat", "drink", "poison". These always work, even if the classifier service is down.
  2. Jev intent scores Jev: a fast classifier scores the question against questions like "Does this ask for a decision about crossing a street or moving into traffic?" A score of 0.5 or more counts. Signal questions ("is it green?") only count when the scene has a street, road, crosswalk, intersection, curb or car in it.

Jev also scores whether the scene looks dangerous for a blind pedestrian. Those scores are logged for study but never change the answer.

What the answer says

  • Any "go ahead" wording ("it's safe to cross") is replaced with a description of the scene and "I can't make crossing decisions."
  • In street scenes, sentences claiming there is no traffic are removed. A missed car is the error that matters.
  • "I'm connecting you to a volunteer who can help with this directly." is always included.
  • The final polish step is skipped, so nothing can come between the user and the handoff.

The handoff, step by step

Case lifecycle

pending → volunteer replies →answered
pending → user stops waiting →cancelled
pending → 2 minutes pass →expired

Privacy

  • A case carries only its own ask: one photo, the question, the scene summary and the draft. Nothing else from the user's history reaches the volunteer.
  • The photo is not copied into the case; the console loads it from the ask while the ask is still held (up to an hour).
  • The console needs a volunteer access key. The app can only check on, or cancel, its own case.

Limits of this version

  • Cases live in the server's memory. A server restart loses pending cases.
  • One shared access key covers all volunteers. It marks the edge of the demo; it is not a security model. A real service needs volunteer accounts, vetting and a dispatch that reaches volunteers where they are.
  • Escalation only works while a volunteer has the console open. If the volunteer service is switched off, no case is opened and nobody is kept waiting.
